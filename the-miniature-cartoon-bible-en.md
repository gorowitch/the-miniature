# The Miniature Cartoon Bible - English Edition

Version en-0.4 (draft)

This is the English edition of "De Miniatuur". It is a supplement to the Flemish bible (`the-miniature-cartoon-bible.md`) and does not repeat it: characters, costumes, cameras, poses, board states, colours, jokes, production rules and all images are those of the Flemish bible and the Flemish album. This file holds only what differs in English: the English text of the play, the English text in the images, the English names and terms, and any panel where the English text needs a different balloon layout.

Author decisions for the English edition (en-0.1):
- Claude drafts the translation; the author corrects it.
- Standard English. The original was written as a play: the translation keeps each character's own way of speaking (the King pompous, boastful and quick to curse; Alfil formal and deferential; Regina practical and level-headed; Caballo proud and fussy about his felt).
- Chess terms follow standard English chess terminology (English piece letters K, Q, R, B, N in the moves; "rank", "hanging", "fork", "opposition", "triangulation", "underpromotion").
- The names of the characters stay as in the Flemish edition (Koning is "the King"; Alfil, Regina, Caballo unchanged).
- Title: The Miniature (A Modern Farce in One Act).
- The images and scenes are used as they are: no redrawing.
- The English album is a second PDF alongside the Flemish one. The English edition lives in `comic/english/` (a copy of the code in `comic/flemish/`, preview server on port 8769); its PDF is `comic/english/pdf/the-miniature.pdf`. Only texts differ from the Flemish edition; drawing changes are made in both.

# E1 - FULL PLAY, ENGLISH TRANSLATION (DRAFT, awaiting author review)

The Miniature (A Modern Farce in One Act)


King:
What a joyful day this is. Behold my troops: from rook to rook they stand in perfect battle order, ready for this brave fight. No wonder "The One Across the Board" is already starting to tremble. For I can feel it in my felt: this glorious game I shall win today! Counsellor Alfil, are our pawns ready for battle?

Alfil:
Indeed, Sire, they await your command!

King:
Then let us tarry no longer: glory and honour await us. Charge! 1. e4
(audible grumbling from the second rank)

Regina:
Well? Why isn't anything happening?

King:
Alfil, my counsellor, you know that as king I do not speak to that lowly peasant folk. Ask those lazybones on the second rank what the matter is. And be quick about it, because "The One Across the Board" naturally thinks we are scared and is probably laughing at us already.
(Alfil confers with the pawns and comes back)

Alfil:
Mutiny, Sire! The pawns refuse to advance to a central square, because apparently there is a bump in the board there. If they have to march uphill the whole time, they will be exhausted before they have even joined the battle. If you wish to win the game today, you will have to play something else.

King:
Who, goddamn it, is actually king around here? Alfil, spare me the drivel. I know full well that back in the day you would rather have been promoted to a queen, and that underpromotion complex is still playing on your mind, but I will have 'no namby-pamby nonsense' here. Go out and see what's going on with that bump, and make sure those peasants take the centre "sito presto".

Alfil:
Forgive me for contradicting you, Sire, but for the time being I cannot get out. After all, both e2 and g2 are still occupied by rebellious peasants.

King:
Well, damn and blast! All my preparation down the drain.

Regina:
Then play e3, Sire, so Alfil can at least go and have a look.


King:
Oh, all right then, because "The One Across the Board" is getting rather impatient.
1. e3
1. ... d5

King:
There you go, see? What did I tell you? "The One Across the Board" is grabbing the centre straight away.

Alfil:
But Sire, with that bump.

King:
Alfil, wipe those paprika crisps off your mitre and get out there.
2. Bd3

King:
Bump, bump. That's a fine way to win the game: "The One Across the Board" gets to play chess while we sit here playing Stratego and aren't allowed into the centre.
2. ... e6

King:
Sir Caballo, jump to f3 so I can castle in a moment.
3. Nh3

King:
Caballo, are you a horse or a stupid donkey? I said f3, didn't I!

Caballo:
Stand on that filthy square, in that greasy spot? Out of the question, Sire! Driving your foot soldiers through it is one thing, but you do not leave an officer to rot with his base in such a filthy, dirty spot. What if it dries and later I can't give a fork because I'm stuck to the board? And besides, you can castle just as well like this!
3. ... Bd7

King: (sighs)
More than a thousand games I have played by now, and never have I seen such nonsense. In the early years, I remember it well, it was still on wooden boards. Hours and hours I played on them. And won a lot, mind you, for those were my younger days. Then all of a sudden a wooden board was no longer good enough and the pieces began to grumble: the varnish is coming off, the dark squares aren't right any more, there are splinters along the edge. Anyway, they refused to play on it any longer, and the sooner the better it had to be one of those modern plastic ones. And worst of all: a new board came, but not three years later they were already grumbling all over again. Those newly promoted officers have no respect at all for good equipment. I'm telling you.

Regina:
But Sire, your officers may well have a point.


King:
Regina, listen: who actually knows this board best? You glide across it far too easily with your long moves, and if you ask me you don't even properly appreciate quality. I, on the other hand, have walked that whole board square by square many a time, used the square, triangulated, taken the opposition. Distant opposition too, mind you. I once even ploughed up that entire board with our two knights to mate "The One Across the Board" using his own last pawn. Heh heh, he didn't like that one bit, I can tell you. And now you act as if I don't know my own board? You'd better go out and keep an eye on that clumsy donkey on h3.
4. Qh5
4. ... Ne7

King:
Could there be a spot on f6 as well? Because the donkey of "The One Across the Board" doesn't seem keen to go there either. Hey, wait a minute … . Sir Caballo, hop to g5. Come on, giddy-up!
5. Ng5
5. ... g6

Caballo:
For glory and victory! Charge!
6. Nxh7

King:
Goddamn it, Caballo, you stupid donkey! How many times must I tell you that I am the one in command during the game. Alfil, don't just stand there gaping, do something. Regina is hanging, surely you can see that yourself? Damn and blast it, "The One Across the Board" is already to move.
6. ... gxh5

King:
There you go, there it is. Regina captured. And while I sit here toiling away to win the game, she will probably spend the whole time beside the board making eyes at that clown of a black rook over there on h8. Pff, the oaf can't even move diagonally; whatever does she see in him.

Alfil:
Forgive me for interrupting, Sire, but I believe we have a mate in one.

King:
Thank you for that information, Counsellor Alfil; "The One Across the Board" won't like that one bit. Sir Caballo, to f6 at once!

Caballo:
Absolutely out of the question, Sire! Someone spilled cola there, and I am not going to stand in that wet muck with my clean felt. If need be I'll capture on f8, because that looks nice and dry. By the way, "The One Across the Board" is offering a draw.

King: (sighs)
Accept it, then.
1/2 - 1/2

## Translation Notes (for the author's review)
- "Dieje van den overkant" becomes "The One Across the Board" (author decision), in quotes and with a capital T wherever it appears, as the original always writes "Dieje" with a capital.
- "boebel" becomes "bump" (also in "Bump, bump.").
- "viltje" becomes "felt": "I can feel it in my felt" keeps the play on "feel it in my bones".
- "boeren" (pawns, and peasants): "peasants", so the pun on pawns as peasants survives ("lowly peasant folk", "rebellious peasants").
- "raadsheer" (counsellor, and the Dutch word for the bishop): "counsellor"; the double meaning is lost in English. To make up for it the King already calls Alfil his counsellor in his first speech (author decision): "Counsellor Alfil, are our pawns ready for battle?"
- "Ridder" Caballo: "Sir Caballo".
- "paard of nen stommen ezel": "a horse or a stupid donkey"; the knight is a horse, so the joke holds. "ezel" stays "donkey" throughout.
- Chess terms: "kwaliteit" is "quality" (author decision, en-0.4: the Dutch word means both quality, which echoes the King's complaint about good equipment, and the chess term for the exchange; English has no word for both, so the King's line keeps "quality", not "the exchange"); "staat in" is "is hanging"; "aan zet" is "to move" ("is already to move"); "vork" is "fork"; "vierkanten gemaakt, driehoekskes gedraaid, oppositie, verre oppositie" are "used the square, triangulated, the opposition, distant opposition" (author decision: "used the square"); "minor promotie" is "underpromotion"; "doorpakken op f8" is "capture on f8"; "met zijne laatste pluspion mat zetten" (two knights can only mate when the defender still has a pawn) is "mate ... using his own last pawn" (author decision); "remise voorstellen" is "offer a draw"; "de zwarte velden" is "the dark squares"; "officieren" stay "officers" (the pieces, as in the Flemish bible); "pasgepromoveerde officieren" are "newly promoted officers".
- Moves in English notation: Ld3 Bd3, Ph3 Nh3, Ld7 Bd7, Dh5 Qh5, Pe7 Ne7, Pg5 Ng5, Pxh7 Nxh7; pawn moves and 1/2 - 1/2 unchanged.
- Curses: "godverdoemme" is "goddamn it"; "nondedju" and "dju miljaar" are "damn and blast (it)"; "gene zever" is "spare me the drivel"; "gene slappen kak" is "no namby-pamby nonsense", kept in single quotes as in the original.
- "sito presto" is kept as the King's own (garbled) Italian, in quotes as in the original.
- "chips" is "crisps".
- Punctuation follows standard English (no space before "?", "!" and ":"); "wait a minute … ." keeps the original's trailing dots.
- Stage directions: "(audible grumbling from the second rank)", "(Alfil confers with the pawns and comes back)", "(sighs)".
